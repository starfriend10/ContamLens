Workflow

Methodology

Turn publication data into the topics, trends, domains, and interactive visualizations.

01

Publication retrieval

Collect relevant publication metadata from Environmental Science & Technology.

Publication records from 1967–2026 were retrieved from Web of Science. Research articles, reviews, perspectives, and relevant editorials were retained after duplicate and irrelevant records were removed.

Publication year, title, keywords, abstract, document type, affiliations, corresponding-author address, DOI, and other bibliographic information were retained.

02

Keyword cleaning

Standardize terminology before topic analysis.

Scientific keywords frequently appear as spelling variants, abbreviations, formulas, synonyms, and singular or plural forms.

A six-step workflow removed excess components, unified acronyms, standardized chemical names, combined principal terms, corrected special cases through expert review, and applied terminology stemming where appropriate.

03

Contaminant identification

Identify contaminant- and stressor-related topics.

Candidate terms were screened using automated chemical recognition, contextual rules, and expert review.

Related expressions were consolidated into canonical terms so publications discussing the same contaminant or stressor could be analyzed together.

04

Trend analysis

Compare normalized topic frequencies across time.

Topic popularity was normalized by publication volume so periods with different numbers of papers could be compared fairly.

A modified trend factor compares normalized frequencies between past and current periods. Positive values indicate increasing attention, while negative values indicate declining attention.

05

Associated metadata

Process temporal and geographical information.

Publication years and author affiliations were processed to characterize temporal and geographical research evolution.

Country and region information was extracted using rule-based parsing, named-entity recognition, and post-processing. Corresponding-author affiliations were prioritized when available.

06

Bottom-up classification

Organize topics into a multilevel hierarchy.

The identified topics were organized through AI-assisted drafting and expert refinement.

The final structure includes up to five hierarchy levels, 18 domains, and four thematic panels. Branch depth varies according to the conceptual structure of each topic.

Temporal comparison

Trend factor

Topic frequencies are normalized by the total number of publications in each period and reported per k publications. The trend factor compares the normalized frequencies of a later period, Pj, with an earlier period, Pi.

F period = ∑ start = n end = m f period ∑ start = n end = m N period ( if n ≠ m ) = fn Nn ( if n = m ) F Pi = k × ∑ P n,i P m,i f Pi ∑ P n,i P m,i N Pi and F Pj = k × ∑ P n,j P m,j f Pj ∑ P n,j P m,j N Pj ( Pj > Pi ) Trend factor = log ( F Pj F Pi )
Trending-up TF > 0.5 Fcurrent > 1
Emerging TF > 1 Fcurrent > 2
Trending-down TF < −0.5 Fpast > 1
Legacy TF < −1 Fpast > 2

Keyword-level

15-year consecutive periods

P1 1967–1981
P2 1982–1996
P3 1997–2011
P4 2012–2026

Fifteen-year periods reduce annual variability and emphasize long-term topic evolution.

Domain-level

5-year rolling windows

1967–1971 → 1968–1972 → 1969–1973 → 1970–1974 → …

Five-year windows use the starting year as the timestamp, reducing keyword noise and clarifying domain-level evolution.

Frequency definitions

Aggregated and unique frequencies

Aggregated frequency Counts every topic–publication link. One paper may contribute to multiple topics.
Unique frequency Counts each publication once within the selected topic group.
faggregated = Σt=1x Ptopic t
funique = ∪t=1x Ptopic t

Interaction

Common controls

  • Hover over visual objects to reveal detailed information.
  • Click legend items to hide or show categories.
  • Double-click a legend item to isolate one category or restore all.
  • Use the chart mode bar where zooming, panning, resetting, or exporting is enabled.

Interpretation

Reading visual encodings

  • Larger objects generally indicate greater publication frequency.
  • Color may indicate domain identity or temporal trend direction.
  • Normalized frequency supports comparison across periods with different publication volumes.
  • Absolute frequency reflects the original topic-publication count.
